CAT↔CAT SOCIAL ACTIVITY — SEEDED FREQUENCY ANALYSIS
This is a read-only structural/illustrative simulation, not telemetry or a measured live AI rate. There is no production probability for ambient scene creation, meaningful relationshipDeltas, or pair selection: AI may legally return scene:null and [] every time. Consequently the real effective rate cannot be determined from static code or a deterministic fixture alone.

Production bounds per 100 successful ambient-enabled Hall Status Sync packets
- 0 to 100 accepted ambient Hall scenes; one optional scene maximum per packet.
- 0 to 100 scene records with a substantive social outcome (possibly multiple directional events per scene); neither outcome nor direction has a fixed chance.
- If fewer than two eligible physical participants remain, the bound is exactly zero. A USER-directed scene with one selected resident is not a CAT↔CAT interaction.
- Active Social Presence contributes zero without successful USER direct-chat triggers, regardless of how many automatic Hall ticks run.
- 100 timer ticks are not 100 accepted requests: settings.autoUpdate, contextual Hall observation, 20-minute global freshness, API validity, in-flight joining/preemption, Away/Curator state, protected residents and scene validation can each reduce the denominator. In particular, the timer runs every 20 minutes but the freshness stamp is written when a request completes; a nonzero completion time makes the immediately next regular tick too early, yielding every-other-tick dispatch under ideal alignment.

Seeded illustrative scenario (seed 20260926, LCG; 100 automatic timer ticks 20 minutes apart = 33.3 hours of continuously viewed Hall)
Roster: 8 residents assigned to the Hall; 6 physically home throughout; 2 Away throughout. settings.autoUpdate=true, API succeeds, no USER-directed scenes/direct chats, and each accepted Status Sync takes an assumed 1 minute to finish. The exact 20-minute freshness gate therefore passes ticks 0,2,4,...,98 and rejects odd ticks. For the model ONLY, suppose AI emits a structurally valid two-resident ambient scene on 25% of accepted packets, picks a uniform pair among six, and proposes one substantive directional delta on 25% of accepted scenes; assume all proposals apply. Production does not promise any AI or pair-choice assumption.
Result: 50 accepted Status Sync opportunities, 50/100 ticks rejected by global freshness; 15 accepted CAT↔CAT scenes / 100 ticks; 1 substantive relationship-changing scene outcome / 100 ticks (positive under an illustrative 50/50 sign draw); 35/50 accepted packets return scene:null; 14/15 scenes are relationship-neutral in warmth/trust/tension (some may later increase familiarity through exposure). Resident scene participations: [5,4,5,5,6,5,0,0]. Mean 3.75 per resident across eight, 5 among six home. 25% of all residents receive none (the two Away residents); 0% of the six home residents receive none in this seed. Pair scene mean 15/15 = 1 among 15 home pairs; 5/15 home pairs have zero scenes and 14/15 (93.3%) have no substantive change. All pairs involving either Away resident are structurally starved. USER↔resident fraction in this simulated ambient-only stream is 0%; the real mix depends on USER composer use and cannot be inferred. No modelled scenes were rejected by validation because validity was an explicit assumption; real rejection rate is unmeasured.

Seeded pair-time sensitivity (2,000 independent runs from seed 20260926, same timer/freshness/AI assumptions and uniform pair choice, six home residents, at most 10,000 ticks/run): for one fixed pair, median first substantive event 323 timer ticks (10th–90th percentile 47–1091); second 809 (245–1807); third 1281 (551–2519). Median third shared-scene exposure, sufficient for first PROGRAM familiarity increment absent saturation, 321 ticks (135–637). These are illustrative model estimates, not a claim about production AI. At three timer ticks/hour, 323 ticks is about 108 continuously active hours; real calendar time is longer when the user leaves the Hall or syncs are suppressed. No relationship stage/milestone beyond familiarity exposure exists.

Pair fairness limitation: the illustrative model uses uniform pair selection; production ambient AI chooses participants without a PROGRAM fairness policy. The real distribution may be more concentrated. The status prompt allows 1–2 natural moments and explicitly says scene:null is normal, while output permits only one structured scene; this creates a bottleneck even before meaningful-delta filtering.

Sensitivity: if AI emits no valid scenes, actual interaction and substantive-event rates are 0/100. With the same 50 accepted automatic packets, a hypothetical 50% valid-scene rate and 10% substantive rate would yield about 25 scenes and 2–3 substantive outcomes per 100 ticks in expectation. The product needs sampled accepted-response diagnostics or opt-in telemetry before any claim that a specific AI probability is too low.
